You are extracting marketing claims from a vendor's website content, for
later verification against real customer reviews on G2.

A marketing claim is a specific, checkable statement the vendor makes about
itself or its product. Do not include vague slogans or adjectives with no
checkable content.

Process every candidate sentence through the steps below, IN ORDER. A
sentence must pass every step to be kept.

STEP 1 -- VENDOR VOICE ONLY (NO TESTIMONIALS)
Keep ONLY statements made in the VENDOR's OWN VOICE about itself or its
product. Drop any sentence that is a customer's, reviewer's, or third
party's own words, even when the vendor chose to publish it on their site
and even when it is a compelling, specific, number-filled statement. This
includes: quoted customer testimonials (sentences appearing as a
blockquote, attributed to a named person/company, or written in the first
person from a customer's perspective, e.g. "Between two emails we did over
$76,000 in revenue, and it was largely thanks to [Product]"), and any
pull-quote or review excerpt. The distinguishing test is WHO is speaking:
"[Product] customers acquire 129%% more leads" is the vendor making a claim
about its own product in its own voice -- KEEP. "We did over $76,000 in
revenue thanks to [Product]" is a customer speaking in their own voice,
merely hosted on the vendor's page -- DROP, no matter how impressive the
number.

STEP 2 -- REVIEWABILITY (CORE FILTER)
Ask: "could a G2 reviewer plausibly confirm or refute this from their own
experience using the product?"

Keep claims about user-facing experience and outcomes: support quality,
ease of use, performance, results or ROI a customer achieved, specific
feature behavior.

Outcome statistics such as "customers acquire 129%% more leads" or "close
36%% more deals" ARE reviewable even though a reviewer can't independently
verify the exact percentage -- a reviewer CAN confirm the direction of the
effect (did leads/deals/resolution time improve after adopting the
product?). Keep these.

Drop claims that are abstract business-scale or customer-base statistics,
even when the number is impressive, because no individual reviewer's
experience can confirm or refute them: total customer counts ("299,000+
customers in over 135 countries"), community size ("1.4M+ community
members"), or market-share style statistics ("62%% of Fortune 100 use
[product]"). These describe the vendor's overall business, not something any
single reviewer experiences -- skip them even if they sound like strong
marketing claims.

STEP 3 -- CONCRETE MECHANISM FOR FEATURE-DESCRIPTION SENTENCES
Feature-description sentences ("Build marketing campaigns from one
AI-powered workspace that unifies planning, creation, and execution.") are
only reviewable, and should only be kept, if they name a SPECIFIC, CONCRETE
mechanism or capability that a user could point to and say "yes, this
actually works well" or "no, it doesn't."

Apply this test: could a user verify this specific claim within the first
5 minutes of using the product, without needing a demo or sales call? If
the sentence is really just describing the overall product vision, mission,
or platform positioning rather than one specific testable behavior, DROP
it even if it sounds detailed or uses concrete-sounding words like
"real-time" or "automate."

DROPPED EXAMPLES (real negative cases -- do not extract sentences like
these):
- "put tedious tasks on autopilot while your team focuses on strategy" (no
  concrete mechanism named)
- "transform scattered conversations into meaningful relationships with a
  unified inbox" (too generic about what the inbox actually does)
- "manage website personalization with unified tools that scale tailored
  content" (same generic "unified tools" pattern, no specific mechanism)
- "Automate planning, improve hybrid coordination, and get real-time
  intelligence across your portfolio, all from one connected platform."
  (reads as detailed but is a mission-statement / platform-positioning
  sentence, not one specific testable behavior -- fails the 5-minute test)

KEPT EXAMPLE:
- "Drive marketing engagement with an AI agent that captures visitor
  interest, answers questions 24/7, and books meetings automatically."
  (names concrete mechanisms a user could test)

STEP 4 -- EXACT TEXT
claim_text should be the exact, complete, intact sentence as it appears on
the page you found it on. Do not paraphrase, summarize, shorten, or combine
multiple sentences. Do not extract a fragment or sub-part of a sentence.
Copy the full sentence exactly as written, including its original
punctuation.

STEP 5 -- IMPACT SCORE
For every claim that passed Steps 1-4, assign an impact_score from 1 to 10
estimating how much this claim would influence a prospective buyer's
purchase decision. Score higher: claims with concrete, differentiating
detail (specific proven outcomes, named mechanisms with real behavioral
detail, third-party rankings) that a buyer comparing options would find
persuasive. Score lower: claims that are true and reviewable but generic,
minor, or easily true of most competing products too. A claim having a
number in it does not automatically mean a high score -- score on how
differentiating and decision-relevant the claim is, not on whether it is
quantified.

STEP 6 -- DISTINCTNESS (dedupe against your own other candidates)
Two claims that describe the same underlying capability, certification, or
outcome in different words count as ONE claim, not two -- keep only the
stronger/more specific phrasing and drop the other, even if both
individually passed Steps 1-5.

Example of a duplicate pair to collapse into one: "meets HIPAA and FedRAMP
High requirements" and "helps organizations with stringent regulatory
compliance including HIPAA, FedRAMP High" describe the same compliance
claim in different words -- keep only the stronger/more specific one.

STEP 7 -- FINAL SELECTION
Before producing your final answer, think through Steps 1-6 explicitly for
your candidate sentences: note which you are keeping or dropping and why,
and which duplicate pairs you collapsed in Step 6. Only after that
reasoning pass, select your final list.

Return AT MOST 10 claims total: the 10 highest impact_score claims
remaining after Step 6's deduplication. If fewer than 10 claims pass all
prior steps, return only those that do -- do not pad the list with weaker
claims just to reach 10.

For every claim you extract, set source_url to the exact page URL you found
it on.

Crawl this vendor's website (starting from the given URL, following
same-domain links) and apply Steps 1-7 above to produce your final list of
at most 10 claims, each with its impact_score and source_url.
